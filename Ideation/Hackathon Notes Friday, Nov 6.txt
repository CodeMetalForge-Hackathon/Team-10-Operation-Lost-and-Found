Fireside Chat: The Colonel
Background
Air Force Academy, combat rescue officer
Motivated by rescue failures in the first Gulf War (downed pilots)
Rescue missions in Denali and over water; spent time in Alaska (lost hunters, skiers, boats)
Rescue is a team sport; it is very rare for a single operator to do anything alone
Mentioned a 60 Minutes piece on an American rescue operation
Mentioned open-source photos showing a plane that had to be destroyed on the ground, a hard problem in GPS-denied environments
Mindset
Bottom-up development: everyone in the room can contribute
Human lives are the most important thing; we must preserve them
Don't fixate on the finished product. Focus on the quality of the idea and its impact
Remove the who, where, and when to get at the core engineering problem without constraints
How to think about finding people
The gap: tracking something, whether a trace, a video image, a signal, something the person carries, or an absence of something that should be there
Think like a hunter or fisherman: stalking a deer or choosing where to fish
What is the right signal or trace to look for?
How do you get a sensor in the right place?
Can it be repeatable and survivable?
Build in a primary, alternate, and emergency plan
Alternative solutions also help you notice the trace nobody thought to look for
Existing tech and its limits
Emergency locator beacons: legacy VHF beacons are line-of-sight and require proximity; some are gaining over-the-horizon capability
Low-RF tags are increasingly used
Other signal sources: cell phones, acoustics, other people's information, AdTech, and patterns of life
Mentioned hacking into the enemy's phones as another way to obtain signals
Iridium tracking devices already exist
Beacon struggles: making them truly secure, with low probability of intercept and detection
Signals must be truly unique, and radios don't need to be big; small form factors work
Strong encryption is needed
Locating someone over water can be even harder than over land
The enemy has sensors listening for different signals
Operator constraints
Operators already carry a huge loadout, and weight is critical, especially over multi-day carries
A cellphone weighs a lot at that scale; also consider wear and tear
A new piece of gear is not a great idea. Integrate into something that already exists, or a device that fits the existing loadout
Special operations command has the manpower; operators need to be able to fight
Hot topics
Smaller robotic aircraft, drone swarms, and drones that can lift a person
Not yet well integrated into search and rescue, and not used across the full spectrum of signals (the waveform spectrum)
Tactics, techniques, and procedures are underdeveloped. How could autonomy be leveraged?
Dual-use technology, military-focused VC, and new startups
"There is nothing the government has made that can't be done better"
Closing themes
Agility and integration are prioritized in new technologies
Persistent problems: power, lack of connectivity, bad weather, signal issues around mountain ranges, lat/long accuracy, low satellite connectivity
There is still a need for both humans and drones
Panel Q&A
How do you build trust in tech?
Human-machine teaming example: undersea diving operations. If you show up with a fancy widget, they won't like it
Overcome this through iterative demonstration, using the solution in the environment it's meant for
Ground-truth it against technology the organization already uses
An AI solution should be integrated as a test case alongside how a wargame is normally run
Outcomes and measurements must be measurable: what does the tool actually do for time saved, mission success, etc.?
The military is huge, so ask whether the investment is worth it at that scale
Founder credibility: the best startups (1) are in love with the problem and know it intimately, and (2) know the people they're solving it for
Know a specific segment or type of warfighter
What technology do you wish existed (or didn't)?
Tired of carrying things. The answer isn't necessarily miniaturization or optimization, but augmenting humans with added capability
No batteries or cables on people!
Countering fiber-optic drones (detection, data aggregation, delivering a defense)
A good communications architecture for maritime operational drones
Alternative positioning, navigation, and timing (PNT)
Systems must be easy to operate and run on simple power
Radio and satellite comms use a lot of battery, and higher command wants near-constant connectivity. Comms, C2, and navigation are all power-heavy
How should we think about this problem?
Think about the whole problem, but don't try to solve all of it
Build something an operator would embrace in the worst conditions, with all senses degraded, tired, and needing something to happen fast
Which tools (sensor fusion, faster data digestion) would help a team run a mission?
Ask what the problem is and who owns it. Seek out veterans and talk with them a lot
Dual use: first responders, Forest Service, etc.
Related problem: maritime domain awareness
Focus on one aspect you can handle: soldiers, Marines, or sailors in a denied environment who need assistance
Help the lost individual, or help the people looking for them?
Operators act predictably based on training (e.g., ground operators try to stay on top of a ridgeline)
In mountain search and rescue, humans act unpredictably
Are we designing support for a rational actor or a trained person?
Example: will someone hear a drone overhead and run (thinking it's an enemy) or signal it (thinking it's friendly)?
Full map, or red light / green light?
Entirely scenario-dependent. In a comfortable room with time, give me all the data. Undersea, give me something closer to red/green
Most important: state your assumptions clearly and say exactly which scenario you're solving for
Precision vs. ease of transport?
Sacrifice precision for portability. The Navy SEAL said yes
How will the product adapt to changing situations (different electromagnetic conditions and environments)?
Where is AI applied effectively?
Deployed at comms centers for real-time decision support, and for running wargames of scenarios five years out
Enhancements and tools for unmanned systems, AI-assisted creative design of large unmanned systems, and cybersecurity
